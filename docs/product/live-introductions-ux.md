## Objective

Support adult women’s **self-directed desire and willingness to meet men** by giving them a bounded, trustworthy way to assess a man’s warmth, responsiveness, authenticity, and interpersonal fit.

The objective is never to covertly engineer arousal. The experience must not infer arousal, claim to cause desire, or use hidden persuasion to push a viewer toward attention, disclosure, matching, sexual escalation, or an in-person meeting. A woman’s own account of comfort, interest, desire, and readiness is authoritative; choosing not to continue is an equally valid outcome.

## Evidence synthesis and required sequence

The concept follows a four-layer sequence. This is a safety and validity hierarchy, not a conversion funnel.

1. **Trust, safety, privacy, and autonomy first.** Adult eligibility, an understandable room boundary, authentic participation, clear conduct expectations, private choices, pacing control, and a penalty-free exit must be credible before attraction is asked to carry any weight.
2. **Responsiveness and intimacy second.** The room should help viewers notice listening, care, curiosity, humor, specificity, and respect. These relational cues are more strongly supported than spectacle or sensory stimulation.
3. **Optional viewer-local romantic wording third.** Gentle chemistry-oriented helper wording may be available only as an explicit viewer-local preference that can be changed before use. The setting itself stays private, but text the viewer actively submits may be seen by the facilitator and, if selected, shared without authorship. It must never autoplay, intensify by default, create a shared atmosphere, authorize targeted live flirting, or imply consent to sexual content.
4. **Safe meeting transition last.** Interest after a room does not create reciprocal interest, a message channel, meeting, location grant, or consent to physical or sexual contact. Any move toward meeting must be private, mutual, separately chosen, and supported by the platform’s safety rules.

This ordering reflects the evidence hierarchy: lower inhibition through safety and control; support meaningful attention through responsiveness; permit optional romantic relevance; then ask whether the viewer herself feels ready for a safe next step. Ambient or sensory tactics such as music, lighting, or “mood” effects are not primary mechanisms and must not substitute for this sequence.

## First-study cohort

For this first study, **verified adult male research participants are the only featured live participants; adult women seeking men are viewers**. In the current wireframe-research phase, every participant and viewer representation remains synthetic and fictional.

This role assignment is a **study cohort variable** chosen to bound the first research question. It is not a claim that women are unsuitable hosts, that men are inherently performers, or that featured participation is a permanent platform-wide entitlement for men. It does not set a product rule for other genders, orientations, or role configurations. Any later expansion requires its own inclusive research question, safety review, and approval.

Eligibility is based on established adult status and the participant’s own stated dating direction, not on inferred sexuality, appearance, or protected traits. If adult eligibility cannot be established, access fails closed.

## Concept thesis and naming

**Live Introductions** is the feature family. An individual room is an **Introduction Circle**.

An Introduction Circle is a finite, scheduled session. It has no more than six viewers, excluding the featured man and independent facilitator. Each session includes:

- one featured man;
- an independent facilitator who is distinct from the featured participant and accountable to the study protocol;
- adult women viewers seeking men;
- a disclosed start, end, purpose, and conduct boundary;
- no automatic contact or meeting outcome.

The design hypothesis is that a calm, facilitated introduction can help a viewer assess authentic interpersonal cues with less uncertainty, comparison pressure, and public performance pressure than a mass livestream. The facilitator protects pacing and boundaries; the featured participant has room to respond rather than continuously perform; each viewer privately decides what the interaction means for her.

The language should feel adult, candid, calm, and invitational. It should not feel clinical, voyeuristic, sexually presumptive, or like a talent competition.

## Why this must not resemble TikTok Live

Live Introductions is a bounded introduction format, not an entertainment feed.

- Rooms are scheduled and finite, with a visible beginning and end.
- Each room has no more than six viewers, excluding the featured man and independent facilitator.
- There is no public live feed, endless scroll, viral distribution surface, or drop-in mass audience.
- There is no creator economy: no gifts, tips, subscriptions, paid prominence, or monetized audience access.
- There are no follower counts, public audience rankings, popularity scores, public reaction totals, or “top host” status.
- Discovery and room ordering must not optimize for watch time, retention loops, virality, or performer popularity.
- Purchases, spending, creator status, and room performance must not influence dating reach, matching, messaging, safety access, or report priority.
- Leaving, declining, or expressing no interest must be immediate, private, and free of loss framing or social penalty.

The relevant outcome is not how long a woman watches. It is whether she can make a more informed, self-directed decision while retaining comfort, privacy, and control.

## Evidence-derived UX principles

### 1. Agency defines success

Design every meaningful transition as a choice, including joining, participating, selecting tone, expressing interest, continuing contact, and considering a meeting. Do not infer a viewer’s internal state from watch time, reactions, camera behavior, or silence. “No,” “not now,” and leaving are complete outcomes, not friction to overcome.

### 2. Make trust legible before attraction

Before the social content becomes prominent, make the research status, adult-only boundary, participant roles, facilitator role, room duration, privacy expectations, conduct rules, and exit/report paths understandable. Authenticity cues must be honest and specific; verification must never be presented as a guarantee of identity, compatibility, or safety.

### 3. Keep the room small, finite, and comprehensible

A viewer should be able to understand who is present, why the room exists, what may happen, and when it ends. Do not use surprise guests, hidden audience growth, infinite continuation, scarcity countdowns, or popularity pressure. Predictability belongs in the container even when the conversation feels fresh.

### 4. Design for responsiveness, not performance

Prioritize moments that reveal listening, follow-up, care, curiosity, boundaries, and reciprocal attention. Avoid mechanics that reward volume, provocation, charisma under pressure, sexual boldness, or constant entertainment. The featured participant should not need to manufacture intensity to remain visible.

### 5. Support authenticity without comparison pressure

Use natural, respectful presentation and a low-clutter hierarchy. Avoid beauty-filter pressure, “hot-or-not” framing, public ratings, competitive thumbnails, and visual treatment that turns either the featured participant or viewers into ranked bodies. No interface should imply that polished appearance is proof of trustworthiness.

### 6. Make viewer-local romantic wording optional and explicit

Gentle chemistry-oriented helper wording may be selected for the viewer’s own screen, never assumed for the shared room. The setting remains private and can return to **Neutral** at any time, but question or follow-up text the viewer actively submits is no longer private from Mara; selected read or paraphrased text becomes shared room content and cannot be recalled. Keep all wording within the non-explicit, no-targeted-live-flirting boundary. A tone choice is not consent to any later step.

### 7. Put novelty in conversation, not safety rules

Fresh topics and playful discovery may support interest, but adult checks, room roles, privacy signals, consent language, conduct boundaries, and safety actions must remain stable and recognizable. Never create excitement by making identity, audience, recording, contact, or location conditions unpredictable.

### 8. Let meeting readiness emerge privately

Do not turn room attendance into a public vote or immediate conversion prompt. Give the viewer space to reflect privately on trust, comfort, interest, and readiness. A possible meeting is the end of a sequence of separate mutual choices, never the reward for staying engaged.

## High-level journey

The journey is:

**Research frame → adult gate → reusable neutral defaults → scheduled circle choice → circle-specific boundary and preference confirmation → facilitated introduction → private reflection → mutual continuation → safe meeting readiness**

### 1. Research frame and adult gate

State that the experience is research, identify the synthetic/fictional phase, explain the cohort roles, and establish adult eligibility. A future real-user study would also require approved research consent and protocol disclosures. Failure to establish adult eligibility ends the journey.

### 2. Choose a scheduled Introduction Circle

Show enough information to make a deliberate choice: the circle’s purpose, featured participant, facilitator, topic, start and end, and bounded audience context. Joining is planned rather than induced by a public live feed or urgency mechanic.

### 3. Set boundaries and preferences

Reusable neutral invitation defaults may be set before a circle is chosen, but they are not a commitment to any circle. Before entry to the selected circle, let the viewer review and confirm those preferences, change the degree of participation or tone, understand what is private or visible, and locate leave, block, and report actions. No preference may silently broaden disclosure or imply romantic or sexual consent.

### 4. Enter a facilitated, finite room

The facilitator establishes the purpose, roles, conduct expectations, and time boundary. The format creates a calm opportunity to observe and, where voluntarily chosen, participate. It does not expose popularity signals or encourage a featured participant to chase engagement.

### 5. Assess responsiveness and fit

The experience gives viewers enough paced, meaningful interaction to notice how the featured participant listens, responds, handles difference, and respects boundaries. The product does not label those cues as proof of character or tell a viewer what she should feel.

### 6. Reflect privately

At the room’s close, shift away from performance and toward the viewer’s own assessment: comfort, trust, interest, and whether she wants any next step. Her response remains private unless she deliberately sends an allowed interest signal.

### 7. Continue only through mutual choice

A viewer’s interest does not open unilateral contact. Before a connection opens, either person may decline or withdraw pending permissions. After it opens, **Unmatch** is the sole non-block withdrawal and **Block** is stronger. Room participation never grants access to profiles, messages, media, sexual intent, or location.

### 8. Reach safe meeting readiness

Only after mutual continuation should the experience offer a separate readiness checkpoint for an in-person meeting. The checkpoint reinforces that reciprocal interest is not consent to meet, share location, record, touch, or have sex. The journey ends at an informed readiness state; it does not automatically schedule a date or exchange a location. Any future meeting or location support remains subject to separate consent and its applicable release gates.

## Explicit non-goals

This concept does not include:

- covert arousal engineering, subliminal persuasion, inferred desire, or claims that the product can create desire;
- a TikTok-style public live feed, endless live discovery, viral distribution, or watch-time optimization;
- a creator economy, gifts, tips, subscriptions, paid visibility, or popularity-based dating advantage;
- public attractiveness scoring, public reaction totals, competitive host rankings, or audience leaderboards;
- anonymous random chat, surprise participants, hidden audience expansion, or unbounded rooms;
- public sexual-intent broadcasting, default erotic content, or escalation from romantic tone to sexual consent;
- unilateral matching, direct messages created by attendance, or pressure to continue after a room;
- exact location, precise distance, real-time proximity alerts, automatic meeting pins, or location sharing caused by attendance;
- a claim that the first-study gender roles are universal, natural, or permanent;
- access by anyone under 18 or an exception path when adult eligibility is uncertain;
- replacement of trained human research, safety, legal, privacy, or moderation judgment;
- real-user research, recording, beta release, production release, or store submission under this document.

## Governance boundaries for later design

- **Adults only; fail closed.** No person under 18 may access, appear in, or be targeted through the experience. Age assurance remains a human-approved release gate, not a wireframe claim.
- **Real-user work remains blocked.** Release gates, counsel and privacy acceptance, safety staffing, security review, approved adult assurance, operational report/block/delete paths, market approval, and authentic commit-bound approvals remain required. This document fabricates none of them.
- **Consent stays specific.** Consent to join a room is separate from consent to participate, reveal information, create reciprocal interest, receive contact, message, meet, share media, be recorded, share location, touch, or have sex.
- **Safety actions stay free and prominent.** Leave, block, report, feature-local **Hide Live Introductions**, delete, any canonical app-wide **Emergency privacy**, and appeal entry points may not depend on payment or creator status. Consequential enforcement requires policy-based human review and an appeal path.
- **Private outcomes stay private.** Interest, rejection, romantic preferences, sexual intent, and research responses must not become public scores or social proof.
- **Ordinary private content stays inaccessible to operators.** The concept must not create a backdoor into ordinary profiles, photos, messages, or sensitive questionnaire answers. Any future research observation, recording, or data collection requires a separately approved protocol, explicit disclosure, purpose limitation, and consent.
- **Sensitive data is not an engagement asset.** Dating, sexuality, location, message, photo, questionnaire, and research-response data must not be sold, used for behavioral advertising, or repurposed to optimize popularity or watch time.
- **No system location disclosure is implied.** Attendance, interest, or reciprocal interest creates no dedicated location, distance, meeting-pin, calendar, or contact exchange. Free-form text can still contain sensitive details despite the warning and cannot be guaranteed prevented or recalled. Such disclosure remains prohibited and reportable in this concept. Any future structured meeting pin or temporary location grant must be off by default, pair-scoped where required, explicit, expiring, revocable, and separately gated.
- **Community protections apply.** Coercion, retaliation, harassment, stalking, doxxing, impersonation, fraud, trafficking, commercial sexual exploitation, NCII, recording without consent, and contact after block, unmatch, or applicable pre-open withdrawal are incompatible with the experience.
- **Safety claims remain honest.** Facilitation, verification, moderation, and safety tools can reduce risk but cannot guarantee identity truth, compatibility, screenshot prevention, or an in-person meeting’s safety.
- **Policy status remains truthful.** The community rules are draft and unapproved, and the product remains staging/internal until named human owners accept the required artifacts.

## Wireframe narrative

This narrative is a wireframe specification for the synthetic/fictional first-study cohort only. Every example person, invitation, room, question, signal, and outcome is fictional. It defines screens and copy, not a real-user study, recording flow, location flow, deployment, or release decision.

### Global visual and interaction direction

- **Character:** warm, low-glare, low-comparison, mature, and lightly playful. The page should feel like a well-edited conversation folio rather than a live-entertainment stage or dating-game scoreboard.
- **Palette:** use warm flax (`#F3EFE7`) for the canvas, oat (`#FBF8F2`) for raised surfaces, charcoal (`#292723`) for primary text, stone (`#68635C`) for secondary text, oxidized plum (`#7C5368`) for selected actions, moss (`#536A5B`) for supportive states, ochre (`#94672A`) for caution, and brick (`#9C463E`) for destructive safety actions. Do not map colors to gender. Avoid black-room “after dark” styling, neon seduction cues, glossy skin-toned gradients, or any dark-pattern sensuality.
- **Type and hierarchy:** pair a restrained system serif for names and session titles with a highly legible system sans serif for instructions and controls. Use one clear page title, short section labels, generous line height, and tabular numerals only for clock time. Do not use oversized metrics, streaks, badges, rankings, or conversion language.
- **Signature element:** rotating conversation prompts appear as tactile editorial cards with a small topic tab and ample whitespace. Their playfulness comes from specificity and an occasional gentle turn of phrase, not casino motion, hearts, flames, confetti, or sexual escalation.
- **Human presentation:** the featured participant is a specific person, not a generic male body. The fictional reference participant is **Elias, 29 — a library-program coordinator who repairs old radios and is learning sourdough**. Use one eye-level, naturally lit portrait and concrete interests; avoid cropped body shots, beauty-filter treatment, physique labels, “high value” language, or any body-ranking copy. His status reads “Adult and identity checks completed for this study” with the adjacent qualifier “Verification is not a guarantee of identity truth, compatibility, conduct, or safety.”
- **Viewer presentation:** each viewer has a Mara-only pseudonym, camera/mic off, and independent entry arm. After start, excluded viewers see only personal **Entry window closed** or eligibility failure. Missed-opening cancellation reaches only valid armed viewers and required room roles; zero valid means no viewer recipients. Elias sees no roster/count/presence/reason. Selected anonymous content reveals at least one viewer participated, not who/how many/current presence. C08 recipients see only **This circle is closed.** Delayed/mixed profile eligibility has no Live badge/order/timing cue; residual recognition/inference cannot be guaranteed away, and eligibility stays unavailable without adequate mixing.
- **Facilitator presentation:** the independent facilitator is named and visually distinct from the featured participant; the fictional reference is **Mara — independent facilitator**. “Independent” means she controls the room under the study protocol and is not the featured participant’s co-host. Her session view is limited to session pseudonyms when needed for facilitation, minimum room status, questions, and incident details for this circle; it does not expose ordinary private profiles, photos, standard-discovery decisions, sparks, or messages.
- **Explicit avoid list:** no stereotyped pink/blue gender coding; no public viewer tiles, viewer counts, follower counts, reaction totals, popularity labels, testimonials, scarcity, “most wanted” treatment, attractiveness voting, or social proof; no “rate him,” “hot seat,” “chemistry score,” “upgrade your match,” or comparable body- or desirability-ranking language.

### Consent language used throughout

Every consequential choice carries a nearby **What this choice means** disclosure. Future permission is task-specific and can be declined or withdrawn through a visible control, but withdrawal cannot recall a topic or question already selected or presented, a profile already displayed, text already delivered or read, committed reciprocal interest, or an external capture. It stops only the later use named by that control:

- Joining permits only this viewer to arm entry for one circle under a Mara-only session pseudonym; she may cancel or leave at any time. Every invited viewer decides independently; unarmed, withdrawn, or disconnected viewers neither enter nor block valid armed viewers. Elias receives no roster, entrant count, aggregate-zero, current-presence, arrival/exit, or cancellation reason. Selected content may reveal only that at least one viewer participated. Joining grants no romantic, question, spark, profile, message, media, meeting, location, touch, or sexual permission.
- Selecting **Lightly romantic** changes only viewer-local helper copy, optional question/follow-up starters, and private reflection wording toward gentle chemistry-oriented language. It never changes the shared room, Elias’s or Mara’s behavior, the prompt deck, music, portrait, lighting, pace, imagery, or safety labels. The setting is visible only to the viewer and **Neutral** remains available, but Mara sees any text the viewer actively submits; selected read or paraphrased text becomes shared room content. The choice is not consent to targeted flirting, sexual content, contact, or any later step.
- Sending a question permits the independent facilitator to consider that one-author submission for this circle. ### Live-room composition and calm run of show

Every Introduction Circle is invite-based and finite: one featured man, one independent facilitator, no more than six viewers, and a disclosed duration inside the approved 10–15 minute envelope. This pilot uses one calm, fixed **12-minute session with no extension**. There is no public chat. A six-label segment strip orients everyone without scoring completion or pressuring pace:

- **0:00–1:30 · Welcome & boundaries** — Mara names the roles, privacy state, conduct boundary, accessibility announcements, scheduled end, and penalty-free exit.
- **1:30–3:00 · Human introduction** — Elias shares a specific story about his everyday life rather than delivering a profile pitch.
- **3:00–5:30 · Prompt cards** — Mara makes one core selection from the rotating prompt-card deck. Elias may choose its neutral alternative, use its compact form, or say **Skip this one**; this is the only shared prompt-card selection.
- **5:30–7:30 · Private topic choice and response** — viewers may choose privately, choose **Keep it general**, or skip. Only the selected topic appears; no tally, ranking, voter identity, exact count, or current-presence signal appears. Sharing the selection necessarily reveals that at least one viewer participated.
- **7:30–10:45 · Anonymous question with optional one follow-up** — Mara reads or paraphrases one question; Elias answers; its author privately chooses **That answered it**, **Offer one follow-up**, or **Leave it here**. Mara may relay one bounded follow-up without author identity. The exchange is optional, has no public chat or open microphone, and may be cut for time.
- **10:45–12:00 · Close & exit** — at 12:00 interaction stops. Only Mara-confirmed safety may be C07; unconfirmed/unclassified safety or integrity/control uncertainty is S01, then known neutral is C08, otherwise healthy is C06. No boundary click is required.

The current segment receives a quiet underline and the label **Now**; future segments remain plain text. Times orient rather than count down. If the room falls behind, Mara cuts the optional mediated follow-up first, then shortens or skips optional topic elaboration, then uses the compact form of the one core prompt-card selection. She never rushes or cuts welcome and boundaries, accessibility announcements, a safety pause, or close and exit. The scheduled end never moves and there is no extension. Prompt cards always include **Use compact form**, **Use a neutral prompt**, and **Skip**. No playful mechanic may become public attractiveness voting.

- **Motion / haptics:** atomic commitment replaces the stage once with a static card; no alarm, countdown, social signal, or timing-click feedback appears.
- **Accessibility:** announce automatic neutral closure and the identical participant sentence once; focus untimed closeout. No cause/count is encoded or exact-time action required.
- **Privacy state:** Elias/viewers receive no cause, trigger timestamp, count, aggregate-zero, presence, identity, or blame. Mara alone may see the classified neutral trigger needed for closeout.
- **Failure / exit states:** Mara-confirmed safety at the same timestamp wins C07; unconfirmed/unclassified safety or integrity/control uncertainty wins S01. Otherwise C08 cannot become C06 and suppresses only this circle’s Live-derived continuation.

## UX system behavior

This section completes the conceptual behavior contract for the synthetic, fictional wireframes. **Must** and **must not** are normative UX requirements. They define visible permissions, choices, states, and transitions only; they do not specify streaming architecture, storage architecture, real-user operations, deployment, or release readiness.

### Normative role-permissions model

Access is denied unless a capability is explicitly assigned below. The UX must not provide prohibited direct signals through counts, timing, notifications, disabled controls, accessibility output, or failure copy. It cannot guarantee that a person will never make a residual inference from shared conversation content or later recognize an ordinary profile; those limits must be disclosed rather than overstated.

#### Viewer

- A viewer may enter or withdraw from the preview, pass the adult gate, review one invitation, confirm one circle, independently arm or withdraw only her own scheduled entry, observe if validly admitted, use private helpers, submit bounded inputs, leave, block, report, reflect, send/withdraw her pre-confirmation **spark**, and make each later choice separately.
- Her camera and microphone remain off. Other viewers receive no direct identity, pseudonym, presence, action, or exit signal; ordinary human inference from shared content cannot be guaranteed away.
- She cannot command room open/pause/resume/advance/completion/termination, moderate or inspect peers, speak directly, use public chat, or contact Elias from attendance alone. Her leave remains private; if it is the last valid viewer exit, the system—not the viewer—commits C08.
- She never has to confront the featured participant, explain an exit, answer a facilitator clarification, submit a spark, permit profile eligibility, continue contact, or discuss a meeting.

#### Featured participant

- The featured participant may prepare, appear during the disclosed window, answer or skip a facilitator-provided prompt, answer an anonymous question or decline its one mediated follow-up, ask the facilitator to pause, raise a private concern, or end his own participation.
- He cannot command room open/pause/resume/advance/extension/completion or safety termination, moderate viewers, or request viewer identity. His voluntary **End my participation** may trigger automatic C08 but grants no terminal control.
- He receives no direct viewer roster, identity/pseudonym, armed/valid/entered count, aggregate-zero, lobby/current-presence, arrival/exit, decline, exclusion, start-cancellation reason, question author, private choice, reflection, incoming-spark, profile-eligibility, or viewer analytics.
- Selected anonymous topic/question/follow-up content necessarily reveals that at least one viewer participated, but not who, how many, or whether that viewer remains. Every C08 trigger uses the same participant-facing sentence: **This circle is closed.** He receives no cause, count, aggregate-zero, or presence signal.
- He receives no immediate room-derived candidate list, badge, room order, or Live-linked timing cue. Later profile eligibility must be delayed and mixed among ordinary candidates; residual recognition or inference cannot be guaranteed away, and eligibility stays unavailable if adequate mixing cannot be provided.

#### Independent facilitator

- The facilitator owns **Arm scheduled opening** / **Disarm scheduled opening**, live pause, explicit resume, segment advance/hold, approved cut order, post-end C06 acknowledgment, and safety termination. The system—not an exact-instant human click—enforces the disclosed start/end boundaries. Mara also owns publication of a complete future replacement pair or cancellation.
- Her authority is independent of the featured participant. A request from him does not compel a pause, resume, segment change, or termination.
- She may see only the session-scoped minimum needed to facilitate: eligibility status, session pseudonyms, minimum readiness, necessary accommodation, submitted topic/question/follow-up inputs, a viewer-provided concern or optional clarification, and the private C08 trigger category. That category is never participant-facing.
- She cannot inspect ordinary profiles, ordinary photos, private reflections, sparks, standard-discovery decisions, reciprocal-interest choices, private connection messages, media, location, or unrelated reports. She may not turn session pseudonyms or timing into a viewer roster for the featured participant.
- She may privately remind, pause, explicitly resume with a neutral reminder, shorten optional material, guide close, acknowledge automatic C06/C08, or terminate for safety. She must not publicly identify a trigger or reporter.

#### Study and operator roles

- In this wireframe phase, study staff and operators may inspect only fictional screens, synthetic walkthrough outcomes, protocol conformance, and deliberately created synthetic usability notes.
- A future approved study role could receive only the participant disclosures and measures named in its approved protocol. It would not inherit facilitation powers, ordinary dating access, or permission to repurpose session data.
- Operators do not have access to ordinary profiles, photos, private messages, sparks, reflections, standard-discovery decisions, or pair conversations. Live Introductions must not create an operator backdoor to that content.
- A future authorized safety reviewer may receive only evidence a reporter deliberately submits into a separate, purpose-limited safety case after being shown what will be shared. That exceptional case role is not ordinary operator access and is not implemented or operationally approved by this wireframe.

### Consent and eligibility state model

Every consented capability begins **off / not asked**, is scoped to the named person, room, action, and time, and can be **declined** or have future permission **withdrawn** without penalty. Withdrawal stops later use but cannot recall selected/presented topic or question text, an already displayed profile, delivered/read text, committed reciprocal interest, or external capture. Completion of one choice never preselects, implies, or supplies another. Adult eligibility is a prerequisite status, not consent.

- **Research-preview entry and withdrawal:** entry follows active acknowledgement. Withdrawal exits Live, suppresses future Live invitations/reminders, and cancels pending topic/question/follow-up/spark/profile-eligibility/future-participation states but cannot recall shared/displayed content. Before a thread opens, pending reciprocal/text permissions retain named withdrawals. Once open, only **Unmatch** or stronger **Block** closes it; **Pause messages** only pauses sending.
- **Adult eligibility:** `unknown`, `checking`, `established`, `expired`, `unavailable`, `uncertain`, and `ineligible` are distinct states. Only `established` permits progress. Every other state fails closed, with no consent or facilitator discretion able to override it.
- **Joining one room:** a viewer actively confirms the current participant, facilitator, audience boundary, fixed start/end, and preferences for one named circle. It does not carry to a rescheduled circle, another room, or a changed boundary.
- **Viewer visibility:** first-study viewer camera, microphone, image, name, and presence have no viewer-facing or featured-participant surface. The session pseudonym and minimum status are visible only to the facilitator when needed. Any future viewer-camera or identity visibility would require a separate, just-in-time gate withdrawable for future display; it could not recall an image or identity already shown.
- **Speaking:** viewer speaking and an open viewer microphone have no first-study control. A future speaking mode would require a separate, just-in-time, turn-specific permission withdrawable for future audio; it could not recall audio already heard or captured.
- **Romantic or flirting tone:** `Neutral` is default. `Lightly romantic` is a changeable viewer-local setting for gentle chemistry-oriented helper wording only. The setting remains private, but Mara sees actively submitted tone-shaped text and selected read/paraphrased content becomes shared and cannot be recalled. Live targeted flirting remains unavailable and the non-explicit boundary still applies.
- **Anonymous topic:** each submitted choice may be withdrawn until the private window closes or Mara selects a topic. A selected topic becomes shared, reveals only that at least one viewer participated, and cannot be recalled.
- **Anonymous question:** each send is one author’s separate submission. The author may withdraw until selection. If Mara reads or paraphrases it, the content becomes shared, reveals at least one viewer participated without identity/count/current presence, and cannot be recalled.
- **Anonymous follow-up:** **Offer one follow-up** is a new choice. The author may use **Withdraw follow-up** until selection. Selected read/paraphrased text cannot be recalled; it grants no later permission.
- **Spark:** a **spark** is only the viewer’s sealed action and grants no profile display or later permission. It expires at the exact displayed date/time seven days after normal completion unless withdrawn or reciprocal interest is confirmed. Withdrawal automatically ends dependent profile eligibility and prevents unconfirmed reciprocal interest; an already displayed profile remains seen.
- **Profile eligibility for standard discovery:** after a spark, the viewer may separately allow delayed eligibility during the same seven-day window. There is no immediate handoff; the candidate must be mixed among ordinary candidates with no badge, room order, or timing cue. Residual inference/recognition cannot be guaranteed away. If adequate mixing is unavailable, eligibility stays off. Withdrawal stops future display/use but cannot recall a shown profile.
- **Reciprocal interest:** **reciprocal interest** is a private mutual outcome produced only when the active viewer spark and active, independent featured-participant standard-discovery interest align while profile eligibility remains active. It is an outcome, not consent to messaging or any later action.
- **Text connection:** each person separately chooses pair-only text. Before opening, **Withdraw connection permission** or **Withdraw from reciprocal interest** closes pending stages. Once open, reciprocal interest is historical; **Unmatch** is the sole non-block withdrawal and closes text plus meeting stages, while **Block** is stronger. Pending permission expires at the exact displayed date/time seven days after confirmation with no reminder. No dedicated media, location/contact/calendar, presence/read/typing, or meeting-pin controls exist; free-form sensitive disclosure remains possible and prohibited.
- **Media sharing:** the concept provides no dedicated image, audio, video, file, intimate-media, or disappearing-media control. Any future medium and each send would require a separate, just-in-time permission withdrawable before delivery, approved NCII controls, and clear recipient scope; delivered/viewed media could not be recalled and text permission would not inherit.
- **Meeting discussion and readiness:** V17 opens readiness only by deliberate action; **Not now** sends no state. Each owner sees only their exact expiry. No push/live update occurs while V17/V19 is open. Next deliberate open or dedicated action revalidates both choices; expiry, withdrawal, connection state, or failed validation invalidates both for that cycle. Identical **Meeting readiness is no longer mutual** / **Return to meeting readiness** follows, then both choose afresh in V18.
- **Location and contact:** the concept provides no dedicated/system-generated location, distance, calendar, social-handle, phone/email, meeting-pin, or travel-route control and never prompts for those details. Free-form text can still contain them; prevention, recall, or external-capture control cannot be guaranteed. Such disclosure remains prohibited/reportable without implying filtering or message surveillance. Any future structured location capability requires its own approved gate.
- **Recording:** the concept provides no platform recording, replay, clips, transcript saving, or downloads. A future recording proposal would require a new approved protocol and separate, just-in-time agreement from every affected person, withdrawable before capture begins; withdrawal could not erase an external or already distributed capture.
- **Touch and sex:** no in-app state—including romantic tone, a question, a spark, reciprocal interest, text connection, or meeting readiness—is consent to touch, sexual activity, or recording in person. Consent must be freely given for each person, act, and moment and can be withdrawn at any time; this concept does not attempt to capture or predict it.

#### Atomic withdrawal cutoff for reciprocal interest

- The conceptual cutoff is atomic: **withdrawal wins until reciprocal-interest confirmation commits**. At that boundary, an unexpired spark, dependent profile eligibility, and featured discovery interest must all still be active inside the seven-day post-normal-completion window. Spark withdrawal automatically ends profile eligibility. If any required state expires or is withdrawn first, reciprocal interest remains unconfirmed and no later stage opens.
- A profile already viewed in standard discovery cannot be recalled or made unseen. Before confirmation, withdrawal still removes future eligibility and prevents a new reciprocal outcome without revealing whether display occurred.
- After confirmation but before a text connection opens, **Withdraw from reciprocal interest** closes pending connection permissions without erasing the historical outcome or information already viewed. Once V17 opens, that control disappears: **Unmatch** is the sole non-block post-open continuation withdrawal and closes text and meeting stages; **Block** applies stronger suppression.
- This is a UX ordering and copy rule, not a technical architecture specification. No interface may expose which side of the display boundary occurred or turn a race into a pressure message.

### Room and session state model

The healthy primary path is fixed:

**Scheduled → briefing → independent viewer arms plus facilitator arm → atomic disclosed-start boundary → live segments → automatic disclosed-end stop → resolved healthy C06 → private debrief → optional V12**

- **Scheduled:** a complete pair, one featured participant, one independent facilitator, and six-viewer maximum are disclosed. Each invited viewer independently arms or withdraws V07 entry; Mara separately arms or disarms C02 opening. A replacement pair requires fresh V05/V06/V07 choices.
- **Briefing:** adult eligibility, roles, visibility, conduct, fixed timing, preferences, currently unavailable capabilities, and leave/block/report controls are reviewed. Changed terms invalidate the confirmation.
- **Private lobby:** every invited viewer waits privately and independently chooses **Enter at scheduled start**, withdraws, or does nothing. Mara sees pseudonymous minimum readiness and separately uses **Arm scheduled opening**. No one acts at the timestamp.
- **Automatic disclosed-start boundary:** one atomic check admits every valid armed viewer together. Opening requires Mara’s arm, at least one valid armed viewer, valid role/adult/boundary/integrity gates, and valid adult/connection status for each admitted viewer. Excluded viewers receive only **Entry window closed** or their eligibility failure, never a room outcome. Failed opening notifies required room roles and still-valid armed viewers; zero valid means no viewer recipients. No late entry exists.
- **Live segments:** the only normal live sequence is `0:00–1:30 Welcome & boundaries → 1:30–3:00 Human introduction → 3:00–5:30 Prompt cards → 5:30–7:30 Private topic choice and response → 7:30–10:45 Anonymous question and optional one follow-up → 10:45–12:00 Close & exit`.
- **Fixed-end precedence:** `(a)` only Mara’s explicit safety confirmation committed at/before boundary creates C07; `(b)` an unconfirmed/unclassified safety concern or integrity/control uncertainty creates S01 with system copy; `(c)` otherwise a known neutral trigger creates C08; `(d)` only an otherwise healthy room creates C06; paused/unclassified/unknown falls to S01. Never infer Mara’s action.
- **Normal completion:** automatic C06 occurs only after the ordering above finds no safety, integrity, or neutral trigger and confirms a healthy live room. A known neutral trigger never waits for a facilitator timing click.
- **Private debrief:** each role receives a terminal-specific reflection or closeout and can return to ordinary app navigation. No one is automatically sent into a spark, discovery, connection, or meeting action.

The following branches and overlays are mandatory:

- **Rescheduled:** a complete future 12-minute pair closes the old lobby and invalidates prior room confirmation, readiness, place, and consent. It may be prepared before start or offered after a missed start, but it never converts the old pair into a late opening. Everyone explicitly repeats V05/V06/V07.
- **Cancelled:** before start, global invitation cards may use **Circle cancelled — no action needed**. After the window, excluded invitees see only **Entry window closed** or eligibility failure. Missed-opening cancellation reaches only required roles/still-valid armed viewers; zero valid means no viewer recipients.
- **Viewer disconnected / reconnecting:** before start, disconnection excludes only that viewer, who receives **Entry window closed** on return and no room outcome. It never blocks peers or permits late entry. Live disconnection keeps privacy/leave controls and never pauses/extends the room.
- **Featured participant disconnected / reconnecting:** unknown disconnection pauses for explicit safe resume and becomes S01 if unresolved. A deliberate **End my participation** is a known neutral trigger that atomically commits C08 without waiting for Mara.
- **Facilitator disconnected:** the room pauses immediately and never auto-resumes. Recovery requires Mara’s explicit action before the end; unknown status at the boundary routes S01.
- **Paused:** Mara controls resume. Safety/integrity provenance resolves first and a known neutral trigger resolves second; only a remaining unclassified/unknown pause falls to S01. A pause can never default to C06.
- **Safety-terminated (C07):** exists only after Mara’s explicit confirmation commits at/before boundary. Without it, safety uncertainty is S01; **Mara ended the room** is never automatic.
- **Neutral trigger closure (C08):** featured voluntary exit, last-valid-viewer exit, or another classified neutral trigger commits atomically at its timestamp without a Mara timing click. Mara sees the private cause; recipients see only **This circle is closed.**
- **Integrity closure (S01):** system copy covers integrity/control loss plus unconfirmed/unclassified boundary safety. It never attributes termination to Mara; ordinary navigation remains.
- **Silent viewer exit:** immediate for that viewer, then V11. Other viewers receive no signal. If the exit leaves zero valid viewers, the known last-valid-viewer trigger atomically commits C08; Elias sees only standard C08 copy, never count/presence/reason.

There is **no early/late entry, automatic resume, time extension, or exact-end neutral-close click**. Start failures follow recipient boundaries; end ordering prioritizes safety/integrity, then known neutral triggers, then healthy C06, with unknown state failing S01. Pauses/cuts never move boundaries; optional follow-up, topic elaboration, then compact prompt are cut in order while accessibility, safety, and close/exit remain protected.

### Moderation and room-safety behavior

- **Host policy drift:** “host” in failure-frame shorthand means featured-participant drift; it grants him no host permissions. A viewer may use **Flag a boundary privately** or leave without confronting him. Mara may observe drift directly or receive a session-scoped concern.
- **Private concern:** Mara acknowledges privately and may ask at most one optional, session-scoped clarification. Declining, leaving, or continuing to a report creates no adverse inference.
- **Least-public intervention:** for a correctable boundary miss, Mara gives Elias a private reminder. If risk, ambiguity, persistence, or viewer welfare warrants it, she pauses. She may explicitly resume with a neutral room-wide reminder that identifies no reporter or use safety termination.
- **No public blame:** C05 and C07 may name Mara’s controlled pause/termination; every C08 trigger uses identical cause-neutral participant copy; S01 names system integrity/control loss and never Mara. None names a reporter, alleged target, private reason, or viewer count.
- **Viewer welfare follow-up:** after a concern, silent exit, pause, or termination, the viewer may privately open debrief, block, report, or leave. Follow-up is optional, does not require returning to the room or answering Mara, and does not promise an operational support service in this wireframe.
- **Severe states:** credible coercion, harassment, threat, retaliation, explicit sexual escalation, viewer-identification attempts, social-handle/location requests, recording/capture deception, or privacy breach requires immediate pause. C07 follows only Mara’s explicit confirmed termination; unresolved boundary state is S01.
- **Facilitator loss:** loss of independent control pauses immediately. Neither Elias nor an operator may assume facilitation. Mara may explicitly resume before the end; unrecoverable or unknown facilitator status at the boundary routes S01 with neutral control-unavailable copy.

### Research hypotheses and guardrail measures

These are hypotheses, not claims about women or proof of effect:

- A small, invite-only room with fixed roles, a visible end, private exit, and independent facilitation may improve **comfort** and **trust** compared with an ambiguous mass-live context.
- A paced prompt and one facilitator-mediated anonymous exchange may make the featured participant’s **responsiveness** easier to assess without requiring viewers to perform publicly.
- A response that addresses a viewer’s chosen topic or anonymous question may help her feel **specifically appreciated**, while hidden authorship and absent tallies avoid public comparison or competition.
- Private reflection, equally weighted no/not-now choices, and non-inheriting gates may support **self-directed interest** and **low regret** better than immediate conversion prompts.
- Reciprocal interest, text permission, and meeting readiness may be more meaningful when measured as separate voluntary outcomes rather than one rapid-conversion funnel.
